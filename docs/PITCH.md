# Evidentia: the evidence room for field programmes

*Built for PS02 "AI-Powered Impact & Sustainability Media Platform" (Cloudinary track).*

---

## The 30-second pitch

> NGOs and sustainability teams collect thousands of photos and videos from the field, but when a
> donor asks "prove it", the media sits in WhatsApp groups and Google Drive folders, and the report
> says "142 households now have water" with nothing behind the number.
>
> **Evidentia turns raw field media into verifiable evidence.** Teams upload photos and videos. AI
> describes what each one shows, humans verify it, and reports are written only from verified
> claims. **Every sentence in a report links to the exact photo, the exact Cloudinary transformation,
> the AI model that analysed it and the person who approved it.** Anyone can re-check a published
> report and prove it has not been changed.

One line: **"Every sentence and every pixel traces back to its source."**

---

## The problem

1. **Media is scattered and unsearchable.** Nobody can find "the pipe installation photos from
   Village A in August".
2. **Impact reports are not trustworthy.** Numbers and claims are typed by hand, with no link to
   proof. Donors and auditors have to take them on faith.
3. **AI makes this worse if used carelessly.** An AI writing "the project improved lives" from a
   photo of a pipe is inventing an outcome, not reporting evidence.
4. **Privacy.** Field photos contain faces, children and locations that must not leak into public
   material.

## The core idea: Observation ≠ Claim ≠ Metric

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | What a photo *appears* to show (from AI or a human, with a confidence score) | "Pipe installation visible, 93% confidence" |
| **Claim** | What the organisation *asserts*, backed by verified observations | "Pipes were laid at Village A" |
| **Metric** | A measured number with a declared source and method | "142 households, door-to-door survey, Form HH-7" |

Reports may only use **approved claims** and **sourced metrics**. That rule is enforced by the
software, not left to good intentions.

## How it works (the demo story)

1. **Upload.** A field worker uploads photos and videos. They go straight to Cloudinary, privately.
   Our server decides every upload setting, so nobody can put files in the wrong place.
2. **Understand.** AI reads each photo: capture time and GPS from the camera data, duplicates,
   a caption, activities from the project's own list (e.g. "excavation", "tank construction"),
   visible text, faces. Cloudinary's AI goes first; Gemini and Groq take over if it fails.
3. **Search.** Ask in plain English: *"pipe installation at Site A in August"*. It combines filters,
   text search and visual AI search, and shows *why* each result matched. "Find similar" works from
   any photo.
4. **Verify.** A reviewer approves, rejects or corrects each AI observation. Nothing is overwritten;
   every decision is logged.
5. **Claim.** The team writes claims and links verified evidence. The software blocks a claim that has
   no verified evidence, or that contains a number no metric backs up. A different person must
   approve it (four-eyes rule).
6. **Report.** One click writes the report from approved claims only. Every sentence carries a
   citation; any AI sentence with an unbacked number is automatically thrown out. Limitations
   ("some photos have no camera timestamp") are generated by rules, not by the AI.
7. **Publish and prove.** Publishing freezes the report. The database refuses any later change, and
   anyone can press **Verify** to rebuild it and confirm it is identical. A PDF is stored privately
   in Cloudinary and shared through expiring links.

Plus:
- **Video:** AI splits videos into timestamped segments and transcribes speech. A search can return
  "Video 00:21–00:37", and clicking it plays exactly that moment.
- **Before/after:** the system finds matching photos of the same spot taken months apart, aligns them
  and measures the visible change, with honest limitations. A reviewer confirms the pair.
- **Campaign Studio:** social-media images (1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 16:9) from approved evidence only, with a
  cited statistic. Faces are always pixelated. AI-enhanced images are labelled and can never be used
  as evidence.
- **Share links:** read-only, expiring, redacted views for donors.
- **Map and timeline** views, and an analytics page with AI usage and latency.

## Why Cloudinary is central (important for the Cloudinary track)

| Cloudinary feature | What we use it for |
|---|---|
| Signed, **authenticated** uploads | Evidence is private by default; the server signs every upload |
| **Named transformations** + strict mode | Every rendition is a pre-approved recipe, and its exact name is recorded for traceability |
| **Analyze API / AI Vision** | Captions, quality, tagging against the project's own activity list, structured extraction |
| OCR and auto-tagging add-ons | Text in signs and documents (treated as data, never as instructions to the AI) |
| **Face pixelation** (`e_pixelate_faces`) | Mandatory on anything shared outside the organisation |
| Video (`so_`/`eo_` clips, `sp_auto` streaming, Video Player) | Evidence clips and seek-to-segment playback |
| Overlays (`l_`, `fl_layer_apply`) | Before/after composite images |
| Raw authenticated storage + expiring download links | Report PDFs |
| Webhooks + contextual/structured metadata | Keeping our database and the Cloudinary Media Library in sync |

Where Cloudinary has no API (its Visual Search only exists in the Media Library for Enterprise), we
built our own with the SigLIP AI model and pgvector, so search works on any plan.

## What makes it different

- **Traceable to the pixel:** report sentence → claim → observation → AI model and prompt version →
  Cloudinary asset → exact transformation.
- **The AI can't invent numbers:** a deterministic check rejects any sentence whose numbers are not
  sourced metrics.
- **Tamper-evident reports:** published versions are hashed, locked by the database and re-verifiable.
- **Honest before/after:** measures *visible* change and always states limitations. It never claims an
  outcome.
- **Privacy built in:** faces pixelated on every external output; precise GPS only for authorised roles.
- **Multi-organisation and secure:** each organisation's data is isolated inside the database itself.

## Likely questions and answers

**"What if the AI is wrong?"** AI only *suggests* observations. A human verifies every one before
it can support a claim, and every decision is logged.

**"Can the AI make up impact?"** No. Reports use approved claims only, and any sentence with an
unsourced number or no citation is removed automatically. Limitations are written by rules.

**"How do I know a report wasn't edited later?"** Published versions are locked by the database and
hashed. Press Verify to rebuild the report and compare.

**"What about people's privacy?"** Evidence is private in Cloudinary. Anything external gets faces
pixelated, and precise locations are hidden.

**"Does it scale?"** Search over 1,000 assets answers in about half a second under load; uploads go
straight to Cloudinary, so our servers never carry the files.

**"Why not just use Google Drive?"** Drive stores files. Evidentia knows what is *in* them, who
verified it and which report sentence depends on it.
