## Pitch B · 2 minutes

**[Hook: 15 s]**
> "Imagine you're a donor. An NGO sends you a report: *'We cleared 4 drains before the monsoon.'*
> How do you know it's true? You don't. You just trust a sentence."

**[Problem: 25 s]**
> "That's how impact reporting works today. Field teams take lots of photos, but they sit scattered across
> phones and chat groups. Nobody can search them, nobody checks them, and nothing connects a photo to the
> sentence in the report. So reports are just words, and trust is easy to break."

**[Solution: 40 s]**
> "Evidentia fixes this with a chain of trust.
>
> **One:** a field worker uploads photos. They go straight to **Cloudinary**, stored privately.
> **Two:** **Cloudinary's AI** looks at each photo: it writes a caption and tags what it sees, like
> *'drain opening'* or *'worker without protective gear'*.
> **Three:** a person verifies what the AI found. AI is only a suggestion until a human confirms it.
> **Four:** the team writes **claims**, the sentences they want to report, and attaches the photos as proof,
> like receipts on an expense claim. A **second person** must approve every claim.
> **Five:** we generate the report, and every sentence carries a citation to its proof."

**[Why it's different: 25 s]**
> "Three things make it trustworthy. The AI **can't invent anything**: any sentence without a citation, or
> with a number that doesn't match a real record, is deleted automatically. **Nobody approves their own
> claim.** And published reports are **frozen**: one click re-checks that not a single word has changed."

**[Close: 15 s]**
> "It's live, deployed, and built on Cloudinary from upload to PDF. Donors don't have to trust us anymore:
> they can check. **Every sentence and every pixel traces back to its source.** Thank you."

## Pitch C · 5-minute stage pitch (with slide notes)

### Slide 1 · Title (0:00–0:20)

> "Good morning. I'm Abuzaid, and I built **Evidentia**, an AI-powered evidence platform, built on Cloudinary,

### Slide 2 · The story (0:20–1:00)
*Slide: a real drain-cleaning photo + the sentence "We cleared 4 drains."*
> "Let me start with a simple sentence: *'We cleared 4 drains before the monsoon.'* Every year, NGOs and city
> departments write thousands of sentences like this in reports to donors and governments.
>
> Behind this one there were real workers, real drains and real photos. But the donor never sees that
> connection. They see one sentence, and they have to trust it."

### Slide 3 · The problem (1:00–1:40)
*Slide: three problems with icons: Scattered · Unsearchable · Unverifiable*
> "There are three problems.
> **First, the photos are scattered:** on phones, in WhatsApp groups, in random folders.
> **Second, they're unsearchable:** nobody can find 'the photos of Ward 12 from last week'.
> **Third, and most important, they're unverifiable:** nothing links a report sentence to its proof. And now,
> with AI writing reports, it's even easier to produce text that sounds right but isn't backed by anything."

### Slide 4 · The solution: the chain of trust (1:40–2:40)
*Slide: Photo → AI observation → Human verification → Approved claim → Cited report*
> "Evidentia builds a **chain of trust** in five steps.
>
> **Photo:** field workers upload directly to **Cloudinary**, where the files are stored privately and never changed.
>
> **Observation:** **Cloudinary's AI** analyses every photo: captions, image quality, and tags from the
> project's own activity list, like *'drain opening'*, *'safety measures'*, or *'unsafe manual entry'*.
>
> **Verification:** a reviewer confirms or rejects each AI finding. AI output is always just a proposal.
>
> **Claim:** the team writes the sentences they want to report and attaches verified photos as proof,
> like receipts on an expense claim. Any number needs a real source. And a **second person** approves it.
>
> **Report:** the report is written only from approved claims, with a citation on every sentence."

### Slide 5 · Why you can trust it (2:40–3:30)
*Slide: four badges: No invented facts · Four-eyes approval · Tamper-proof · Honest limitations*
> "Four things make this different from just asking ChatGPT to write a report.
>
> **One, no invented facts or numbers.** A rule-based checker (not AI) deletes any sentence without a
> citation, and any number that doesn't match a sourced record.
>
> **Two, four-eyes approval.** The person who writes a claim can never approve it.
>
> **Three, tamper-proof.** When a report is published, it's frozen and fingerprinted. One click rebuilds it
> and proves that nothing has changed since.
>
> **Four, honesty.** Limitations are written by rules, not AI. The report tells the reader what it *can't*
> prove, like photos without camera timestamps. And if a photo shows a worker without protective gear,
> the platform shows that too."

### Slide 6 · Built on Cloudinary (3:30–4:10)
*Slide: Cloudinary features in a grid*
> "Cloudinary is the backbone, not an add-on. We use **signed private uploads** and **webhooks**; **Cloudinary AI**

### Slide 7 · What's built (4:10–4:40)

### Slide 8 · Close (4:40–5:00)

## Key messages to repeat
1. **Chain of trust:** photo → AI observation → human verification → approved claim → cited report.
2. **Cloudinary is the backbone:** storage, AI, transformations, privacy, video, PDFs.
3. **AI can't invent:** citations and sourced numbers are enforced by rules, not by trust.
4. **Four-eyes plus tamper-proof:** a second person approves, and the published report is frozen and verifiable.
5. **Honest:** limitations and bad news are shown, not hidden.

## Words to use / avoid
| Say | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "verified evidence", "traceable", "cited" | "100% accurate", "proves impact" |
| "Cloudinary's AI analyses…" | "our AI knows everything" |
| "the report shows what it can't prove" | "no limitations" |
| "live and deployed" | "almost done" |

**Be careful:** Evidentia makes evidence **checkable**. It doesn't claim the AI is always right, or that a
photo alone proves impact. Saying this yourself makes you *more* credible.

---

## Delivery tips
- **Speak slowly.** Pause after the hook question and after the closing line.
- **Point at the screen** when you say "citation", "Verify" or "Cloudinary's AI found".
- **Practise out loud 3 times**, with a timer. Pitch C must fit in 5 minutes.
- **Look at the judges**, not the screen, for the opening and closing lines.
- **Have a published report ready** in a second tab as a backup.
- **End on the one-liner**, then stop talking: *"Every sentence and every pixel traces back to its source."*
